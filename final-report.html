Rook Dispatch · Final report · 16 Oct 2026

Release 4.2 quietly turned four responders off.

On 12 August a shorter wait and a heavier distance weighting shipped, and a missed ping started costing a responder as much as a turn-down, with no way back. Four of sixteen responders stopped hearing from us. This is how we found it, what we'd build instead, and the check that stops it recurring.

What shipped on 12 August

Two dials turned. Two flaws were left alone.

Release 4.2 changed only one file, config.py. Everything below came from reading the routing code, not from a spec. No written description of how pings are decided existed.

CHANGED

How long a ping waits

90 seconds
60 seconds

Same for every responder. More pings expired before anyone could answer. No document records why.

CHANGED

Weight on distance

0.45
0.60

Global, so it applied to everyone, including responders who turn jobs down. Whether that was a decision is undocumented.

CHANGED

Weight on recent acceptance

0.40
0.25

Capability stayed at 0.15. Everyone stays on the list; only the order changes.

NOT CHANGED

A miss costs the same as a decline

−0.12

offer.py:30 scores both the same, although the glossary says they are recorded separately.

NOT CHANGED

Nothing ever recovers

No decay

+0.08 per taken ping is the only way up. A 2019 code comment flags the missing decay; still open.

Why it was hard to see

A trap with no exit.

Together the two flaws and the shorter wait make a loop. Responders who missed early pings were scored like decliners and ranked behind near neighbours, so they were asked less, so they had no chance to earn the score back.

The route and amounts come from the code. The four responders' scores come from my replay of history.py over the pings table, not from production scores. Ravi Menon's data will confirm or correct them.

What we'd build instead

Recover on its own. Tell the handler early. Keep a button as the backstop.

The brief rejects a quiet config change (no record of why, which is how this started), a revert of 4.2 (wide-area responders asked for the distance weighting) and a "guaranteed look" (sends callouts to worse-placed responders). The direction is below, and the prototype shows it.

AUTOMATIC
A missed ping costs less than a turn-down. A turn-down is a decision; an expiry may be a phone in a pocket.
AUTOMATIC
Standing eases back over days. Today it takes about 12 taken pings to climb back, and a quiet responder can't earn them.
AUTOMATIC
A higher floor. Today a score can fall to 0.0. The exact value is for Ravi's data to set.
VISIBLE
The handler is told early. "Meteor Mite has gone quiet. 1 ping this week, usually 12."
BACKSTOP
Restore their standing. One logged action. If handlers need it often, the automatic rules are wrong.

How it stops happening again

A six-point check, run the same way every time.

4.2 shipped without a written description of how pings are decided, with no named owner for the weight change, and a headline metric that hid four people. The review-checklist skill asks the questions that would have forced those gaps into the open. Point it at any brief and get the same verdict, table and fixes.

The "4.2 question" on each card is my reading of what the check would have asked. It is not a finding that a 4.2 brief existed or failed.

1

Owner

Names a specific person or role, and who decides.

4.2 question: who chose the new weights, and were they meant to cover decliners? Still unanswered.
2

Success

A metric with a baseline, target, date, reader, guardrails and a stop rule. Nine questions, including "does it measure the problem?"

4.2 question: would an acceptance headline have caught four responders going dark?
3

Scope matches

What the brief ends up covering matches what it said at the start.

4.2 question: three commitments on the roadmap, two shipped. Where did Availability Confidence go?
4

Problem before fix

Who is affected and what happens to them, before any solution.

4.2 question: release notes say wide-area responders asked for it; the handover says nearby ones went unoffered. Which problem?
5

Customer experience

The fix says what the customer sees or gets, not config values.

4.2 question: what does a responder experience when their phone quietly stops ringing?
6

Teams involved

Every team named, with a specific ask, a contact and where they stand.

4.2 question: what ties the routing change to the analyst who owns the numbers, and to support?
